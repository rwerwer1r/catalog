# Examine competing explanations and choices

Use when [framing](frame.md) established at least one material alternative and the [routing index](INDEX.md) selected examination.

1. **Choose only warranted lenses.** Examples include empirical correctness, end-user outcomes, operations, security, cost, accessibility, reversibility, and future maintenance. Name the specific decision question each lens tests; do not assume every example applies.
2. **Represent each live option fairly.** State its strongest evidence-backed case, relevant assumptions, limits, and feasible counterexample. Where evidence is asymmetrical, reflect that asymmetry rather than forcing equal plausibility or airtime.
3. **Try to disconfirm material claims.** Check primary evidence or targeted validation when available. Label direct evidence, inference, conditional scenarios, speculation, and unknowns separately. Do not fabricate sources, measurements, stakeholder preferences, or test results.
4. **Challenge the critique itself.** Before retaining a counterargument, ask whether it is distinct, supported or testable, proportionate to the outcome, and not already refuted. Reject strawmen and performative dissent.
5. **Stop on saturation.** Another perspective or round must have a credible route to changing the decision or its confidence. Do not force an additional round, role, or opposing argument just to appear thorough.

Optional multiple agents or isolated contexts can help on material questions when authorized and available; never call sequential self-questions independent review. Return only decision-relevant arguments and uncertainties to [resolve.md](resolve.md).
